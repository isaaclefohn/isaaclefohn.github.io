Method and full write-up for the portfolio study

Method and full write-up for the bank comparison

Setup: Three publicly traded banks with heavy Oregon and Washington exposure: Columbia Banking System (COLB, $66.8B in assets, post-Umpqua merger), Banner Corp (BANR, $16.4B), and Heritage Financial (HFWA, $7.0B, the highest equity/assets of the three). Same geography, different strategies.

COLB is by far the largest, but it has the lowest ROE in the set (7.02%) and a −18.0% five-year total return. The case for it depends on post-Umpqua cost savings showing up in earnings, which gets harder if NIM compresses.

HFWA is the defensive option: the smallest by assets, but the highest equity/assets (13.23%) and the lowest beta (0.48). Its return tracked the benchmark (+10.6% vs. +11.7%). Not a growth story, but a capital cushion if commercial real estate credit weakens.

Approach: Fundamentals come from SEC EDGAR's XBRL companyfacts API (data.sec.gov). Banks report the same item under different XBRL tags, so the script checks a list of fallback tags (first_available()). Market multiples come from Yahoo Finance via yfinance. Estimated NIM is net interest income divided by total assets, an approximation (true NIM uses average earning assets).

Built with: Python (yfinance), SEC EDGAR XBRL API

Method and full write-up for the Banner pitch

Valuation, $82.02 target: (1) Peer P/E re-rate to 13.5× (COLB/HFWA average) → $80.26. (2) Normalized P/E (13.5x community-bank norm) → $80.19. (3) P/B re-rate to 1.5× for a 10%-ROE bank → $85.62. The target is the simple average of the three (+21.1% vs. $67.76). The two P/E methods use the same 13.5× multiple, so the price-to-book method and the separate takeout analysis are the real cross-checks.

Four-part thesis: (1) Highest ROE of the three peers, yet the lowest P/E. (2) Lower execution risk: no major integration underway as of April 2026, compared with COLB's post-Umpqua merger. (3) Equity/assets of 11.9% supports the dividend and buybacks and gives a cushion if credit turns. (4) A 3.09% dividend yield, with a payout under 50% of trailing earnings, pays you while waiting for a re-rating.

Risks & mitigants: Commercial real estate stress (the main risk to watch; community-bank CRE lending has a different profile than big-bank office exposure), deposit outflow (loans were 84% of deposits at fiscal 2025, below COLB's 87%), regional-bank contagion (equity/assets of 11.9%, a thicker capital cushion than the banks that failed in 2023), and NIM compression (it would hit peers too, so BANR's relative position holds).

Approach: The memo follows a standard pitch format: recommendation box (rating, target, upside), situation, numbered thesis, catalysts, valuation table, risks and mitigants, peer metrics, and bottom line. Fundamentals come from SEC EDGAR XBRL filings and market data from Yahoo Finance, using the same code as the Pacific Northwest bank comparison. build_pitch.py lays out the PDF with ReportLab.

Prices as of April 23, 2026. Five-year total returns cover April 2021 to April 2026. Fundamentals are from Banner's most recent 10-K at the time.

Built with: Python (ReportLab), SEC EDGAR XBRL API

Method and full write-up for the Banner takeover cross-check

Why this exists: The BANR pitch reaches its $82.02 target with peer P/E, normalized P/E, and price-to-book. This memo asks a different question: what would a strategic acquirer plausibly pay? Price to tangible book value (P/TBV) is the standard bank-M&A yardstick, so it tests the target from a franchise-value angle instead of an earnings angle.

Finding, convergence rather than extra upside: BANR already trades at 1.46× P/TBV, above the routine-deal multiple used here (1.35×), so the market already prices in some takeover value. The base-case takeout of $71.98 is below the $82.02 standalone target. Only the strategic-premium scenario ($83.59) lines up with the long thesis. Takeaway: a takeover is optionality, not extra upside on top of the pitch.

Why BANR could be a target: (1) Size: $16.4B in assets, large enough to matter and small enough for a regional acquirer to absorb. (2) Franchise: a Washington, Oregon, California, and Idaho community-banking footprint that would be hard to replicate quickly. (3) Profitability: 10.04% ROE and 1.19% ROA, the highest of the three Pacific Northwest peers in the comparison. (4) Capital: equity/assets of 11.9%.

Plausible acquirers (illustrative): GBCI (Glacier Bancorp: Mountain West and Northwest community-bank focus, grows through acquisitions), ZION (Zions: western regional with a relatively small Oregon/Washington presence), FIBK (First Interstate: recent Great Western acquisition), and WAFD (WaFd Bank: recent Luther Burbank acquisition, neighboring footprint). None reflects any actual or reported interest.

Approach: BANR fundamentals come from SEC EDGAR XBRL, using the same code as the Pacific Northwest bank comparison. Goodwill ($373M) is subtracted from equity to get tangible equity, which is divided by shares outstanding for TBV per share. Banner's $1.5M of other intangibles was not deducted (about $0.04 per share). The four P/TBV scenarios (1.20×, 1.35×, 1.55×, 1.80×) are general industry ranges for community-bank deals, not multiples from specific comparable transactions. Outputs: a football-field chart (matplotlib), a three-sheet workbook (summary, acquirers, methodology), and a two-page deal memo PDF (ReportLab).

Caveats: This is a framework exercise, not a deal prediction; no transaction is announced or rumored. Bank Holding Company Act control rules make private-equity buyouts of banks rare, which is why it looks only at bank acquirers. Acquirer accretion/dilution is not modeled; that would require the acquirer's financials, purchase accounting, synergies, and financing mix.

Built with: Python (matplotlib, ReportLab), SEC EDGAR XBRL API

Method and full write-up for the Waste Management DCF

Why WM: Waste Management is a steady, capital-intensive business with a low beta (0.55) and contract pricing that is often tied to inflation. That keeps the focus on valuation mechanics rather than on forecasting volatile revenue.

Approach: Four years of reported financials (FY2022 to FY2025, from WM's 10-K data via Yahoo Finance) as the base. Revenue growth fades from 8% to 5% (Stericycle acquisition tailwind early on), and EBIT margin expands 130 bps, from 17.2% to 18.5%. WACC comes from CAPM (risk-free 4.25%, equity risk premium 5.5%, beta 0.55) plus an after-tax cost of debt, and terminal value uses the Gordon growth model at 2.5%. All drivers sit on one Assumptions tab, so any input can be changed in Excel.

Built with: Python, Excel
